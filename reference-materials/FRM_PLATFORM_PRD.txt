1. Executive Summary
FinFactory EFRM is a modular financial crime and fraud risk-management platform. It receives customer, transaction, 
device, screening, and adverse-media signals; evaluates them through deterministic rules and configurable scoring; 
optionally applies machine-learning analysis; creates operational alerts and investigation cases; maintains risk 
profiles; and gives authorized users controlled workflows for investigation, decision, evidence, audit, and compliance 
review.
The product is designed as a layered system:
1. Detection: Screening, adverse media, transaction rules, device rules, metrics, and ML inference identify signals.
2. Decision: Rules and configured policies translate signals into decisions, scores, priorities, and alert states.
3. Risk: RAMS ingests eligible events and applies configured domain impact, aggregation, decay, overrides, 
relationship propagation, and tiering.

5. Operations: Admin Portal exposes authorized workflows, configuration, governance, monitoring, and review

1.1 Product Position
EFRM is not only a rule engine and not only a case-management application. Its value is the controlled path from a 
source event to an explainable investigation decision:

Source signal
  -> service-specific evaluation
  -> alert/result and risk event
  -> case correlation and investigation
  -> human decision and evidence
  -> configured operational action
  -> audit, compliance, and risk-profile update
1.2 Current-State Qualification
The platform has strong foundations in deterministic rule processing, screening ingestion and evaluation, case 
decision/action orchestration, security capabilities, RAMS processing, and database-backed audit/evidence 
structures. UI maturity is uneven. Rule authoring, screening configuration, user/profile administration, SLA, and 
auto-assignment have stronger UI coverage. Case SOP configuration, RAMS policy configuration, Link Analysis 
configuration, selected shared catalogs, AMC child scoring configuration, and ML governance require further UI/API 
governance before they should be represented as fully self-service capabilities.
2. Product Purpose and Business Outcomes
2.1 Business Problem
Financial institutions need to identify suspicious activity across heterogeneous channels, explain why a signal was 
raised, coordinate investigation, apply institution-specific SOPs, preserve evidence, and demonstrate controlled 
decision-making to internal audit and regulators. Fixed lists and disconnected alert queues create gaps:
 one entity can appear across screening, adverse media, transaction, and device workflows;
 an alert severity is not the final investigator decision;
 client SOPs differ by institution and service;
 machine-generated risk signals and human outcomes must remain distinguishable;
 external action requests require delivery tracking without falsely claiming bank-side completion;
 evidence and audit records must be discoverable and tamper-resistant;
 configuration changes must be governed separately from runtime data.

2.3 Non-Goals
 replacing a bank core banking, card, payment-switch, CRM, recovery, or notification system;
 claiming business completion of a bank-side action without a confirmation contract;
 rewriting original machine-generated transaction, screening, device, or adverse-media result history after 
human review;
 providing an unrestricted client-facing SQL editor or raw secret editor;
 universal workflow-engine functionality, message-broker migration, compensation orchestration, or a visual SOP 
designer;
 treating ML mock/demo pages or placeholder reports as production capabilities;
 changing mature rule-authoring behavior without a specifically approved rule-engine change.

4.3 Shared Data Boundary
Services use a PostgreSQL efrm schema. A deployment may give an institution its own database or share 
infrastructure with strict institution_id isolation. PostgreSQL is the system of record for business configuration, 
runtime records, derived state, audit history, and worker state. Environment and secret configuration remain outside 
ordinary client CRUD.
5. Capability Catalogue
5.1 Transaction and Device Detection
The Rule Engine receives transaction and device inputs through API, GUI/manual, and bulk flows. It normalizes 
source payloads, resolves source-to-engine attributes, loads effective rule groups, resolves metrics/dependencies, 
executes Drools rules, applies score/decision policy, persists results and alerts, and can asynchronously 
create/correlate cases and submit eligible RAMS events.
Status: IMPLEMENTED with client/source configuration dependencies. Mandatory payload fields, source identifiers, 
timestamps, and event fields are contract-specific.
5.2 Screening and Watchlist Management
Screening supports watchlist source configuration and ingestion, source validation, column mapping, list-version 
lineage, entity/alias storage, dynamic entity forms, related entities, fuzzy matching, identifiers, thresholds, risk 
bands, whitelist/suppression, alerts, and operational review. It can integrate with AMC, RAMS, and cases when 
enabled.
Status: IMPLEMENTED for core runtime and ingestion; CONFIGURATION-DEPENDENT for sources, lists, form 
definitions, thresholds, and downstream integration.
5.3 Adverse Media / AMC
AMC accepts an adverse-media request, persists it, acknowledges asynchronous processing where used, resolves 
provider/runtime configuration, searches and retrieves content, extracts and analyzes it, computes decision/risk, 
persists results/matches, creates an alert, and optionally feeds RAMS and cases.
Status: IMPLEMENTED with CONFIGURATION-DEPENDENT provider availability. A transaction/device-only client may 
intentionally omit AMC and screening.
5.4 Rule Authoring and Governance
Rule administration provides rule identity, versioning, authoring/testing, required data, source/engine attributes, 
groups, source bindings, metrics, decision policy, upgrades, and activation/governance. The Rule Engine consumes 
effective versions and bindings at runtime.
Status: IMPLEMENTED and mature for existing rule paths. Unrelated feature work must not change this behavior.
5.5 ML Feature, Model, and Inference Lifecycle
ML supports feature sets and versions, feature definitions/metadata, model registry and versions, training jobs, 
artifacts, live/test/bulk inference, anomaly score/decision mapping, explanations/SHAP where available, feedback, 
and governance/rollout concepts.
Status: Runtime and core configuration are present. Some portal/API governance and lifecycle surfaces are PARTIAL 
or OPEN. Mock-backed pages must not be represented as live production operations.

5.6 Case Management
Case Management is the investigation container. Screening, adverse, transaction, and device alerts can be correlated 
to an existing open case by institution/entity context. case_alert_mapping uses source table plus source alert ID as 
identity so equal numeric IDs from different alert tables coexist.
TEXT DIAGRAM / REFERENCE BLOCK
source alert
  -> case scoring and merge decision
  -> case_master and case_alert_mapping
  -> assignment and SLA
  -> investigation
  -> alert-level finding
  -> case-level outcome
  -> approval when configured
  -> case closure
  -> action queue and dispatch/audit
Status: IMPLEMENTED for case creation, mapping, assignment, SLA, alert-level decision, case-level outcome/action 
orchestration, evidence, audit, and timeline, subject to configuration and source integration state.
5.7 Outcome and Action Orchestration
Client outcomes are stored in case_decision_master. Reusable actions are stored in case_action_master; ordered 
mandatory/optional mappings are stored in case_decision_action_mapping; target endpoint/template/retry policy is 
stored in integration_endpoint_config.
Case closure validates the outcome and required fields, handles approval, preserves alert-level findings, stamps 
unresolved mappings, updates source alerts, persists case decision/status, queues actions, and records audit/events. 
V1 tracks delivery. SENT means the target accepted the request according to configured success criteria; it does not 
assert bank-side business completion.
5.8 RAMS / Risk Rating
RAMS consumes eligible risk_event_log events from transaction, device, screening, adverse, ML, relationship, and 
case-decision sources. It applies event admission, domain/service weights, decision impact, decay, tier, override, 
relationship propagation, aggregation, profile, factor, and history policy.
Case decisions use source_type CASE and event_type CASE_DECISION. Decision-impact configuration can increase, 
suppress, reduce, or override the human investigation signal. Original machine-generated events remain unchanged.
Status: Runtime IMPLEMENTED; policy administration UI is largely BACKEND API ONLY.
5.9 Link Analysis V2
Link Analysis builds configurable entity graphs from source attributes and dimensions, refreshes graph state through 
API/worker paths, stores nodes/edges/evidence/clusters/checkpoints, and presents case/global views. Configuration 
determines dimensions, source paths, relationship semantics, lookback/decay, limits, shared-count logic, masking, 
and refresh.
Status: Operational V2 visualization and refresh are present; configuration UI is DB/SQL ONLY and requires 
controlled administration before client self-service.

6. End-to-End Functional Flows
6.1 Transaction and Device Intake to Alert
1. A client source submits a transaction/device payload using its contract and institution identifier.
2. Rule Engine validates mandatory fields and normalizes source attributes.
3. Source and engine definitions map payload values into canonical facts.
4. Active rule group/version is resolved for institution/source/channel.
5. Metrics and dependencies are resolved under configured limits.
6. Drools evaluates rules and emits signals/matches.
7. Decision policy and upgrades produce decision/risk output.
8. Request/master/result/alert/match records are persisted.
9. Asynchronous case/RAMS paths consume results when configured.
10. Admin Portal reads the alert, signal, case linkage, and risk context.
Core configuration: source_system_master, source_attribute_def, engine_attribute_def, engine_flink_map, 
rule_master, rule_version, rule_group tables, metric_definition, rule_metric_dependency, rule_decision_policy, 
rule_decision_upgrade, efrm_service_config.

6.5 Case Creation and Merge
1. A source alert/result arrives at Admin Service.
2. Source identity, entity identity, alert type, source table, priority/risk, and institution are derived.
3. Case scoring applies policy, alert category, count boost, correlation, and critical override.
4. Open-case lookup is institution/entity scoped.
5. An existing case is reused or a new case_master row is created.
6. case_alert_mapping is inserted using case ID, source alert ID, alert type, and alert source table.
7. Source alert status becomes CASE_CREATED when appropriate.
8. Assignment/SLA state is created or refreshed.
9. Case event/audit lineage is recorded.
6.6 Investigation, Decision, and Closure
Alert-level decisions are mapping findings. They validate context and required fields, persist mapping metadata, 
mirror legacy disposition values, close only the selected mapping/source alert when applicable, and create an 
event/audit record. They do not queue case-level actions or close the parent case.
Final case closure is the official business outcome:
TEXT DIAGRAM / REFERENCE BLOCK
POST /cases/{case_id}/close
  -> validate state, outcome, required fields, and approval
  -> preserve mappings already resolved
  -> stamp unresolved mappings with final outcome
  -> update corresponding source alerts to CLOSED
  -> save case decision and case status
  -> queue configured actions
  -> emit case/audit events
  -> commit transaction

6.7 Action Dispatch
1. Outcome mappings resolve actions in sequence.
2. Endpoint configuration is snapshotted into the queue row.
3. Idempotency key is generated from case, decision, action, and sequence identity.
4. Internal or REST/webhook actions are queued.
5. The dispatch worker claims pending/retryable work.
6. The worker sends the stored request snapshot.
7. HTTP 2xx or configured success codes produce SENT; HTTP 202 may also set business_status to 
AWAITING_CONFIRMATION.
8. Failures create retry metadata and execution logs.
9. Exhaustion/manual resolution remains auditable.

11. Security, Privacy, and Compliance
11.1 Authorization
Authorization is layered:
1. Authentication establishes user or machine identity.
2. Institution context limits data/configuration scope.
3. Profile/menu visibility controls navigation.
4. Capability/endpoint maps protect APIs.
5. Service validation controls business transitions.
6. Audit records preserve actor and operation context.
Portal visibility is not a security boundary by itself.
11.2 Sensitive Data
Customer identifiers, transaction data, source payloads, watchlist data, provider content, evidence files, model 
artifacts, API credentials, signing keys, security data, and action payloads require masking, access control, retention, 
and secure logging.
11.3 Audit and Custody
Audit and custody records are append-only from normal product workflows. Corrections are new events, not silent 
edits. Direct database access is a restricted operational control, not a normal product function.
11.4 Compliance Boundary
The platform supplies evidence/audit/readiness support. It does not replace an institution's regulatory filing approval 
or submission process. STR/CTR recommendations/flags must be distinguished from filed reports.

 RAMS event admission and decision impact match the approved risk policy;

2. Request and Decision Flow
The platform has two distinct decision boundaries:
1. Detection decision: rule, screening, AMC, or ML processing produces a result/alert and an initial machine decision 
or score.
2. Investigation decision: an investigator selects an alert-level resolution or a final case outcome. Only the final case 
outcome initiates configured case actions.

using the efrm application schema. The PRD uses these databases to validate schema/configuration vocabulary and 
client-profile differences; it does not reproduce their operational rows or credentials.

 schema/table presence and model/migration alignment;
 institution-scoped configuration concepts;
 service enablement and client-profile differences;
 runtime/configuration/audit/worker table classification.
